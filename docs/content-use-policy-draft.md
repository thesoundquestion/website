# Content Use & Attribution

We want audio education to be shared. Here is what you can do with our work, what needs permission, and how we handle material from other creators.

**Short version:** Link freely. Credit original work. Ask before republishing substantial portions. Other creators’ material keeps its own terms.

## Using and sharing this site

You are welcome to browse, link to, and share links to The Sound Question. The site exists to make audio concepts easier to understand, and we want its lessons to be discussed and shared.

You may quote brief excerpts of our original writing or show a small extract of an illustration for commentary, teaching or discussion, provided you identify **The Sound Question** and link to the original page. Please keep the meaning intact and avoid implying that we endorse your work.

Guides and handbooks that we provide as downloads may be saved for personal, non-commercial reading. Unless we say otherwise, please link to the original download page rather than uploading and distributing your own copies.

## What needs permission

Please contact us before republishing substantial portions of lessons or handbooks, mirroring the site, selling our written material or graphics, putting them into a commercial training package, or presenting our work as your own.

We may grant permission for particular uses. A separate licence or credit note on an individual page, file or project takes priority over this general policy. Nothing here removes rights or exceptions provided by applicable law.

## Research, references and outside material

We refer to manufacturer specifications, published measurements, articles, research and other creators’ work to explain audio concepts. A citation or link identifies a source; it does not mean we own that source or have permission to redistribute all of it.

Trademarks, product names, photographs, screenshots, recordings and other material belonging to third parties remain subject to their owners’ rights and applicable licences. Visitors should consult the original source before reusing that material.

Where original calculations, example response curves or illustrations are shown, we aim to distinguish teaching examples from real product measurements. Some visual content is made with software or AI assistance; use of those tools does not change the rights attached to third-party inputs or guarantee that every element is independently copyright-protected.

## Audio samples and listening demonstrations

Where we publish playable examples, edited clips, stems or listening comparisons, their source and permitted use may be described on that particular page. Permission to listen through our site does not automatically include permission to download, re-upload, remix, sample or commercially redistribute an audio file.

Audio examples that include third-party material follow their own licence or permission terms. We do not claim ownership of the underlying songs, performances or recordings just because we analyse them.

## Corrections, credits and rights concerns

If we have missed a credit, made an attribution error, linked to the wrong source, or used material you believe requires review, please contact us through our [official contact channels](https://thesoundquestion.pages.dev/connect/). Include the relevant page URL, what needs attention, and enough information for us to understand the request.

We will review credible concerns and make appropriate corrections, add credits, restrict material or remove it where warranted. A request does not need to be written in legal language to receive attention.

## A few important distinctions

Facts, formulas, ideas and audio-engineering principles are not owned by The Sound Question merely because we explain them. Our original wording, selection and arrangement of material, and original creative work may have copyright protection where the law provides it.

This page explains our general sharing and attribution preferences. It does not replace Canadian copyright law, fair dealing or other applicable legal rights. The website is maintained as an independent educational project, not as an official representative of the manufacturers or artists it discusses.
