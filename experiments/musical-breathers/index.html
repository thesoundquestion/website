Lesson 03 · After explaining bass roll-off

Friends in Low Places / Rolling in the Deep

Your favourite speakers may have friends in low places, but that doesn't mean they're capable of rolling in the deep. You might still hear the kick-drum attack and follow the bass line even as the very lowest notes fade away. The response curve helps show how much output remains farther down.

A cohesive bass-extension example placed after the technical explanation, not in place of it.

Lesson 08 · Listening implications of distortion

Coming Together / Good Vibrations / Crowded House

The volume goes up, the drums hit harder, and suddenly things aren't quite coming together. The guitar is still there, the vocals are still there, but the space between instruments seems to disappear. You wanted good vibrations, not a wall of sound that turns everything into a crowded house. Is the amplifier actually adding distortion, running out of clean headroom, or is something else happening?

Describes a plausible listening symptom without treating a subjective impression as proof of a fault.

Lesson 25 · After introducing floating-point recording

I Will Always Love You / Take It to the Limit

Think of the leap from the gentle opening to the powerful later passages of I Will Always Love You. If the recording gain was set only for the quiet part, the big notes might take it to the limit before the take is safely captured. A clean signal can be turned up later; peaks already clipped by the mic preamp or converter cannot be restored just by lowering the file.

Uses a familiar musical dynamic to explain why headroom must accommodate the loudest moment.

Lesson 22 · Speaker jobs and listening distances

Rock and Roll All Nite / We Will Rock You

Maybe you want to rock and roll all night in your living room. Maybe you're setting up a venue where the crowd expects to hear We Will Rock You all the way at the back. Those jobs call for different listening distances, audience coverage and clean output, even when the music is the same.

A quick comparison of a living-room stereo versus sound reinforcement.

Tools · Before choosing a goal

All About That Bass / I Want It That Way / Should I Stay or Should I Go

Maybe you're all about that bass, maybe you want it that way with a simple two-speaker setup, or maybe your current amplifier has you asking: should I stay or should I go? Those are different questions with different answers. Choose the goal you're actually working toward; you don't need the audio jargon to get started.

The music references connect three distinct user goals without changing the form or its logic.
