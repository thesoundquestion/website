# B01 — Speaker dispersion and room paths

Revision 1.0 · 2026-11-11. Original synthetic teaching figures for The Sound Question.

The SVG files are editable vector masters and web-ready artwork. Place the SVGs directly in a vector-capable print layout with their lesson captions and keys. The 720-unit viewport does not limit print resolution. Artwork, technical overlays and seat markers are generated separately in `scripts/render_b01.py`; regenerate from the website checkout with `python3 scripts/render_b01.py`.

- **SPK-01** uses one room and matched speaker aiming. The horizontal contours are illustrative half angles of 27° and 8° at 8 kHz, truncated at the seat row. The fill represents angular coverage and does not give a dB scale. The plan view uses a stretched projection for legibility. Angles are calculated in room coordinates.
- **SPK-02** compares two synthetic traces at a nominal horizontal angle of 30°, each relative to its on-axis response. For octave index n = log₂(f / 250 Hz), the smooth trace is −2 − 0.65n dB. The uneven trace adds a Gaussian-shaped −6 dB dip centered on n = 4 (4 kHz), with a standard deviation of 0.32 octave. The examples illustrate single-angle spectra and supply no full directivity data.
- **SPK-03** uses image-source geometry, with both speakers at (±1.1, 1.2) m, center listener (0, 3.9) m and walls at x = ±2.4 m. For the left path, the image source is (−3.7, 1.2) m and contact is approximately (−2.4, 2.149) m. The difference between the 4.58 m bounce and 2.92 m direct route is about 4.85 ms at 343 m/s. Both paths are plan-view calculations. Line weight does not represent level.

The [lesson](/decoder/speaker-dispersion/) supplies alt text and captions. Technical context: [KLIPPEL Near Field Scanner documentation](https://docs.klippel.de/db-lab/latest/directivity-roomcorrection/nfs/nfs.html) and the [Spinorama measurement guide](https://www.spinorama.org/docs/01_intro.html).
