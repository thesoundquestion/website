01

What do you want to hear?

Who are you building this system for? Your own listening habits matter more than somebody else's ranking. Pick your top priority, then optionally add up to two supporting goals. Music genres don't secretly decide your equipment.

Also important (optional, maximum two)

Choose up to two extra goals if you care about more than one thing. We'll explain where the goals support or compete with each other; we don't pretend to calculate an exact percentage of “sound quality.”

02

Which route fits your space?

A working setup needs more than a promising speaker model. Passive speakers need an amplifier. Powered speakers have amplification built in, but check whether the price covers both speakers and whether you need a volume control or other connections.

Open styles leak sound. Closed and in-ear designs can help with isolation, but real fit and frequency response are model-specific. We do not rank headphones from price or impedance alone.

This tool helps compare system options; it does not calculate your actual volume at the seat or tell you a listening level is safe. Use System Check for an estimate of amplifier power and musical peak reserve with passive speakers. Some Builder products aren't in its preset list yet, so enter verified specifications manually if needed. That passive-speaker calculation isn't suitable for powered PA systems or headphones.

What can you keep?

Enter anything you already own or want to keep. The results build around those components first, or explain why your chosen system type cannot reuse them directly. A model name never proves compatibility. An adapter isn't always a substitute for the right kind of input or output.

What's happening with the bass?

Choose a stereo pair on its own or add a subwoofer for the lowest notes.

A high-pass filter turns down the lowest notes in the main speakers. Check how each setup routes the bass. Read about bass extension.

05

Set the buying constraints

A bargain on one component isn't much of a bargain if the rest of the system can't use it. Budget applies to the complete missing signal path, not just the price on a speaker box.

Use your budget to plan what you need. Add up current prices for the missing parts, delivery and taxes before deciding what fits. Budget, mid-priced and premium are retail cost positions, not audio-quality grades.

Retail-price weighting uses only verified, current evidence for the selected region. Unpriced models remain visible.

A FEW IDEAS FOR YOUR SYSTEM

Systems worth exploring

How these examples are chosen and what the limitations are

Facts, not vibes

Equipment preset claims link to their source where available. Sensitivity bases, load ratings, peak versus continuous output, and frequency-response tolerances matter.

Unknown is not a zero

Unmeasured directivity, clean bass output, room behaviour or used pricing is neither a failing score nor a hidden advantage.

We link, not copy

Independent reviews are useful for checking how real equipment behaves. We link to original measurements, explain their conditions, and keep the reviewer’s work at its source. We do not scrape or reconstruct restricted measurement databases, and a source link does not imply a partnership. See our content-use approach.
