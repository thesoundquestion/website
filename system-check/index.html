Listening goal

80 dB SPL average at your seat is a starting point. Genelec uses a 70–80 dB SPL listening range for room calibration. Edit either level and the other follows. The default 15 dB gap is a starting estimate. A heavily compressed track can have smaller peaks, while a dynamic recording can have larger ones. Change the gap if you know your music. More on peak-to-average levels

What does that level sound like?

For scale, CDC examples put normal conversation around 60 dB, movie theaters around 70–104 dB and rock concerts around 95–115 dB. Distance, the room, and the sound itself change these figures. A short 105 dB drum hit describes a peak. An average describes the whole passage.

Fine-tune the estimate
Room and speaker load

Stereo pair assumes a single-speaker sensitivity figure. Choose +0 dB if the figure already includes the pair. Stereo summation varies with the recording, frequency and seat.

Keep room adjustment at 0 dB when using published in-room sensitivity to avoid counting the room contribution twice. A single-speaker in-room figure can still be used with the stereo pair setting.

Source and amplifier connection

How the estimate works

Sensitivity tells us how much sound the speaker makes at a known input. The calculator estimates how much level you lose with distance, then works out the voltage and watts needed per channel. Room reflections, bass management, speaker compression, impedance phase and the duration of a peak can all change what your system delivers. Published bench ratings apply under their stated test conditions.
