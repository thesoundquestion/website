LISTEN FOR

Small clues, not huge changes.

01
Cymbals and shakers

Does a shimmering sound feel smooth, or a little swishy?

02
Quiet little details

Can you still follow the softer sounds behind the main instruments?

03
The end of a sound

Does a ringing note fade naturally, or seem to change as it disappears?

If everything sounds the same, that's a useful result too. Good MP3 files can be very difficult to distinguish from lossless music.

WHAT DO THOSE WORDS MEAN?

The simple explanation

▥

Lossless

The audio is kept intact. FLAC saves space without throwing away any of the original sound information.

≈

MP3

The audio is made smaller by removing or simplifying some information. A good MP3 may still sound almost exactly the same.

⇄

WAV vs FLAC?

They can contain exactly the same sound. FLAC simply packs the digital information more efficiently.

The real question isn't which file has the bigger number. It's whether the difference matters to what you hear.

For curious listeners: how we made this comparison

The same fixed FLAC excerpt is decoded once and the MP3 versions are encoded from that audio without a different mix, volume boost, EQ or mastering step. Both MP3 examples are constant-bitrate stereo files at 44.1 kHz. Switching keeps approximately the same point in the passage. The browser encoder and decoder can introduce tiny timing differences, so this review is for sighted A/B listening, not a validated blind scientific test.

For the final public version we will pre-encode the samples, verify their levels and timing, then test them on mobile browsers. No conclusion about hearing ability should be based on this early preview.
