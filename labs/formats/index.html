LISTENING LAB / MUSIC FORMATS

Can you hear the difference?

Two versions of the same music. One keeps the full digital recording. The other makes the file smaller. Can your ears tell them apart?

No technical knowledge needed. Your phone, computer or headphones are enough to try it. Headphones can make small differences easier to notice.

LISTEN FOR

Small clues, not huge changes.

01
Cymbals and shakers

Does a shimmering sound feel smooth, or a little swishy?

02
Quiet little details

Can you still follow the softer sounds behind the main instruments?

03
The end of a sound

Does a ringing note fade naturally, or seem to change as it disappears?

If everything sounds the same, that's a useful result too. Good MP3 files can be very difficult to distinguish from lossless music.

WHAT DO THOSE WORDS MEAN?

The simple explanation

▥

Lossless

The audio is kept intact. FLAC saves space without throwing away any of the original sound information.

≈

MP3

The audio is made smaller by removing or simplifying some information. A good MP3 may still sound almost exactly the same.

⇄

WAV vs FLAC?

They can contain exactly the same sound. FLAC simply packs the digital information more efficiently.

The real question isn't which file has the bigger number. It's whether the difference matters to what you hear.

For curious listeners: how we made this comparison

Both MP3 examples are derived from the same 44.1 kHz lossless source, using LAME constant-bitrate encoding at 128 and 320 kbps. There was no separate mixing, EQ or mastering. The files were checked for timing alignment and sample levels. At 128 kbps the decoded sample RMS was about 0.44 dB lower than the lossless source, so we slightly lower the reference and 320 kbps playback to match the 128 kbps sample level. This is an approximate RMS-matched guided comparison, not a blinded scientific test. Switching moves to approximately the same point in the music.

One short listening test cannot prove that everyone will hear or prefer the same format. Listening conditions and the recording itself can change the result.
