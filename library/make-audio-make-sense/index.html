Make Audio Make Sense

Understand the terms you encounter from recording to listening, and learn what an audio specification can tell you.

1. Recording, mixing and mastering

A microphone turns changes in air pressure into an electrical signal. Its placement and pickup pattern affect the balance of the source and room sound. A mix combines recordings and shapes their relative level, tone and position. Mastering prepares the finished mix for distribution and can affect its level and dynamics.

Dynamic range describes a span between levels. For a recording, specify which levels or measurement method you mean. For playback hardware, a specification typically compares a maximum signal with the noise floor under stated conditions. Clipping occurs when a stage exceeds its available range and truncates peaks.

2. Digital audio and the source

Sample rate is the number of samples per second per channel. In a properly band-limited system, a 48 kHz rate can represent frequencies below 24 kHz. Practical filters need transition space. Bit depth sets the number of available quantization levels in integer PCM. It affects quantization noise and usable dynamic range.

Lossless compression reconstructs the original encoded audio data. Lossy encoding discards or approximates information to reduce size. Audible differences depend on the encoder, settings, material and listener. Compare versions derived from the same master if you want to isolate the codec.

A DAC converts digital audio into an analog signal. Its line output feeds a compatible input. Compare the source's maximum output voltage and the receiving input's permitted level when investigating overload.

3. Amplification and headroom

An amplifier provides voltage and current to drive a load. Impedance describes how a speaker or headphone opposes alternating current and can change with frequency. Headroom is the margin available for signal peaks before a stage reaches its clean-output limit.

Worked example. If an amplifier supplies 20 V RMS into an ideal 8 Ω resistor, it delivers 50 W (20² ÷ 8). The same voltage into an ideal 4 Ω resistor requires 100 W and twice the RMS current, 5 A instead of 2.5 A. This calculation assumes the amplifier can sustain 20 V into either load. A real loudspeaker's impedance and phase vary with frequency.

Read a wattage rating alongside its load, distortion threshold, frequency range and number of driven channels. For a distortion figure such as THD+N, check test level, frequency and measurement bandwidth before comparing equipment.

4. Speakers, headphones and the room

Frequency response shows how output level changes with frequency under a defined test setup. Sensitivity relates input to output sound pressure level under stated conditions. Read the voltage or power reference and the measurement distance before comparing speaker sensitivity. Headphone figures may use dB SPL per volt or per milliwatt.

Dispersion describes how a speaker's output changes with angle and frequency. A seat off to the side receives a different angle of direct sound. The walls also receive sound from off-axis directions and reflect some of it back. For headphones, fit and seal can strongly affect the sound at the ear, particularly bass response.

When someone describes imaging, they usually mean how precisely voices or instruments appear to occupy positions. Soundstage describes the apparent extent of that scene. The recording, speaker setup, reflections and listener all contribute.

5. Check what a claim can tell you

  1. Read the conditions. Check the units, reference level, load, angle, distance and bandwidth as applicable.
  2. Identify the evidence. A specification states a result or limit under defined conditions. A measurement records what happened in one setup. A model shows what follows from its assumptions. A listening impression describes what someone experienced.
  3. Match the comparison to the question. If you are testing a codec, use the same master and match playback levels. A blind ABX trial asks whether you can identify a hidden sample as A or B under those conditions. Preference is a separate question.
